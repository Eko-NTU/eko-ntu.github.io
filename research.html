Research

How do information, incentives, and institutions shape economic behavior?

My research uses laboratory and field experiments to study decision-making in markets and social environments. Across finance, market design, cooperation, and human–AI interaction, I examine how the rules and information people face influence individual choices and collective outcomes.

01

Financial Markets and Information

How do information and social connections affect trading and market efficiency?

Experimental asset markets make it possible to vary who holds information, how traders are connected, and which trading rules apply, while holding fundamentals fixed. This work examines information acquisition and sharing in social networks, dark trading, insider trading disclosure, and algorithmic trading, and asks how each affects liquidity, price informativeness, and earnings.

Published work

  • Costly Information Acquisition, Social Networks, and Asset Prices: Experimental Evidence

    with Edward Halim and Nilanjan Roy · Journal of Finance, 2019

    FindingCommunication among traders crowds out costly information acquisition, as traders free ride on their neighbors' signals. Information sharing raises trading volume and liquidity but does not improve price informativeness; net earnings and welfare are higher.

  • How Dark Trading Harms Financial Markets

    with Edward Halim, Nilanjan Roy, and Yan Wang · Economic Journal, 2025

    FindingThe effect of dark trading depends on how information is distributed. When it is concentrated among a few investors, dark trading lowers the quality of asset prices; when it is diffused, price discovery is unharmed, but unobserved liquidity leads to welfare loss.

02

Market Design and Organ Donation

How can allocation rules and information encourage donation and improve welfare?

Organ shortages make the design of allocation rules consequential. Combining theory and laboratory experiments, this work studies how rules that give registered donors priority on waiting lists interact with what people know when deciding whether to register, and how such rules can be designed to raise donation rates without creating unequal incentives across individuals.

Published work

  • Incentivizing Organ Donation under Different Priority Rules: The Role of Information

    with Mengling Li · Management Science, 2025

    FindingWhich rule works best depends on information. When patients' valuations of transplantation are unknown at the time of the decision, dual-incentive rules raise donation rates; when they are known, these rules create unequal incentives that can weaken their effect.

  • Boosting Donation and Welfare: Augmenting Donor-Priority Rule with Information Provision

    with Min Fang and Mengling Li · European Economic Review, 2026

    QuestionCan pairing a donor-priority rule with information provision raise both donation and welfare?

Cooperation and Social Networks

When do social relationships and institutional rules help groups cooperate?

Groups often do better when members cooperate, yet cooperation is fragile. This work studies how network structure, the freedom to choose partners, reputational information, punishment, leadership, and social identity shape cooperation and coordination, using experiments in which participants interact repeatedly and can sometimes change whom they interact with.

Published work

  • Cooperation and Punishment Mechanisms in Uncertain and Dynamic Social Networks

    with Edoardo Gallo, Tat-How Teh, and Nilanjan Roy · Games and Economic Behavior, 2022

    FindingReputational uncertainty significantly decreases cooperation. In fast-changing networks, uncertainty makes participants more lenient when punishing by cutting links, which hinders the formation of cooperative clusters.

  • Highly Flexible Neighborhood Promotes Efficient Coordination: Experimental Evidence

    with Tat-How Teh · European Economic Review, 2020

    FindingGreater freedom to change partners significantly improves coordination when groups start in a decentralized circle network, but the improvement is limited when they start in a centralized star network.

AI and Economic Decision-Making

How do people respond to AI advice, prediction, and automation?

A newer line of work asks how people respond when prediction and advice come from AI rather than from humans. Ongoing projects compare large language models with human forecasters, test whether AI advice improves decisions and how disclosing the advisor's identity matters, and examine whether generative AI helps groups provide public goods.

Ongoing research

Prosocial Behavior and Behavioral Design

What motivates giving and honesty, and how do design choices change them?

This work studies charitable giving and honest behavior, and how seemingly small design choices, such as payment methods, public recognition, framing, and the salience of costs, change them. It combines laboratory experiments with online and natural field experiments, and includes related research on incentive design in contests and on deterrence.

Published work

  • Cash vs Contactless Mobile Payment: The Effect of Payment Modes on Donation Behaviors

    with Jingping Li and Fang Liu · Economic Inquiry, 2026

    FindingIn a real donation setting in the lab, contactless mobile payment led to higher donations than cash; the authors suggest that familiarity with the payment mode plays a significant role.

  • The Effects of Winning Experience and Gain/Loss Frames on Lying Decisions: A Lab Experiment

    with Fang Liu · Applied Psychology, 2026

    FindingWinning experience increases lying in a loss frame but decreases it in a gain frame, a pattern linked to a heightened sense of deservingness after winning.
